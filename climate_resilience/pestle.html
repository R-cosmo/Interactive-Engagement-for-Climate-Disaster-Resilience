PESTLE Explorer

The PESTLE Explorer helps users compare climate resilience and disaster recovery practices through Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental dimensions. It supports structured learning across Australia, the APEC region, and global contexts.

Compare by Dimension

Select a dimension or region to focus the examples shown below.

PESTLE Framework Dimensions

P Political

Focuses on governance, national strategies, intergovernmental coordination, emergency management roles, and public leadership during recovery.

Example indicator: Existence of disaster risk reduction strategies.

E Economic

Examines recovery funding, insurance, infrastructure investment, household support, business continuity, and long-term economic resilience.

Example indicator: Availability of recovery grants and adaptation funding.

S Social

Considers community networks, public education, inclusion, mental health, local knowledge, and support for vulnerable groups.

Example indicator: Community preparedness and participation programs.

T Technological

Reviews early warning systems, mapping tools, data sharing, digital platforms, communication systems, and innovation in resilience planning.

Example indicator: Use of alert systems and risk mapping tools.

L Legal

Looks at planning rules, building standards, emergency powers, risk disclosure, insurance regulation, and responsibilities during disaster response.

Example indicator: Climate-aware land-use and building regulations.

E Environmental

Explores ecosystem protection, nature-based solutions, biodiversity, water security, heat risk, bushfire risk, floodplain management, and restoration.

Example indicator: Use of nature-based adaptation measures.

How to Use This Explorer

  1. Choose a PESTLE dimension to focus on one type of resilience factor.
  2. Compare Australia, APEC, and global examples in the matrix.
  3. Use the indicators as prompts for research, discussion, or stakeholder engagement.
  4. Check official and current sources before using any information for policy or planning.
